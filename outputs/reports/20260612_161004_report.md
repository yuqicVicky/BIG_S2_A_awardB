# Analysis Report — Run 20260612_161004

## 1. Executive Summary

This run modeled `rate_per_10000_ed_visits`, a continuous panel-regression target
covering emergency-department visit rates by month (`period_id`), U.S. jurisdiction
(51 levels), and overdose category (8 levels). The training table
`data/train/dose_sys_train.csv` contains 31,416 rows and 4 columns
(`period_id`, `jurisdiction`, `overdose_category`, `rate_per_10000_ed_visits`),
joined with `data/train/covariates.csv` (3,927 rows, 12 columns) on
(`period_id`, `jurisdiction`).

The selected model is a stacked blend dominated by gradient-boosted trees
(`stack(catboost_log+tuned(catboost)+catboost)`, the round-2 gbdt specialist
candidate, OOF `block_mae`=0.7865), combined via NNLS with the deterministic
floor, trees, and linear specialists into a final blend with OOF
`block_mae` = **0.784656** versus the floor's 0.800677 (a 2.00% improvement).
A robust-generalization adjustment was applied in the sense that
specialist candidates were screened by a feature-ablation gate and a
3-reviewer consensus (model performance, feature leakage, generalization)
before promotion; no separate `adjusted_robust_score` field was emitted by
`final_model.json` (this artifact does not exist for the specialist
modeling mode — `model_selection.json` / `promotion.json` serve this role).

The primary limitation, drawn from `data_profile.json.summary_warnings`, is
substantial missingness in the prediction covariates: `unemployment_rate`
(54.6% missing) and `labor_force` (58.0% missing) in `data/val/covariates.csv`
— both of which were ultimately pruned from the model by the feature
ablation gate.

- `temp_avg_f`, `precip_in`: jurisdiction-month training median.
- `unemployment_rate`, `labor_force`: jurisdiction training last-known-value carry-forward.
- `state_doh_release`: filled with empty string plus a structural-missing indicator
  (`state_doh_release__is_missing`).

**Encoding strategy**:
- `jurisdiction`: per-fold target encoding (target aggregates, see below).
- `overdose_category`: one-hot encoding (8 columns) — present in the full feature spec
  but pruned by the ablation gate (see below).

**Datetime-derived features** (`period_id`, detected as

`period_id__year`, `period_id__month`, `period_id__quarter`, `period_id__month_sin`,
`period_id__month_cos`, `period_id__day`, `period_id__dayofweek`, `period_id__is_weekend`,

all retained by the ablation gate (`feature_audit_review.json`: datetime_derived delta

**Text features**: `state_doh_release` was processed via TF-IDF (max_features=2000,
1-2gram, min_df=2) → TruncatedSVD (20 components: `state_doh_release__svd_0..19`)
plus the missing-indicator, fit on full train text and applied to prediction text
(21 columns total, retained — `text_svd` ablation delta = -0.001097, decision = keep).

**Per-fold target aggregates** (`per_fold_aggregates`, 4 columns, retained — delta
= -0.001621, decision = keep):
`jurisdiction_category_target_mean`, `jurisdiction_target_mean`,
`category_target_mean`, `jurisdiction_category_target_std`, grouped on
(`jurisdiction`,`overdose_category`), (`jurisdiction`), and (`overdose_category`).

**Pruned by the ablation gate (`other_features`, 27 columns, decision = prune,
delta = -0.041934 ≈ 4.9× the prune margin of 0.00848)**: all raw weather/economic/
gtrends covariates (`temp_avg_f`, `precip_in`, `unemployment_rate`, `labor_force`,
`gtrends_overdose`, `gtrends_fentanyl`, `gtrends_naloxone`, `gtrends_opioid`,
`gtrends_methamphetamine`), the 8 `overdose_category__is_*` one-hots, the
`jurisdiction_x_period_ordinal_rank` interaction, the 3 `overdose_category_*_x_gtrends_fentanyl`
interactions, the experimental lag/rolling target features
(`target_lag1/2_per_jurisdiction_category`, `target_rolling_mean_3/6_per_jurisdiction_category`,
`gtrends_fentanyl_lag1_per_jurisdiction`, `cross_category_auxiliary_lag1_signal`).
The `feature-engineering-reviewer` confirmed this prune as "a large, unambiguous signal,
not a modest round-1 prune" since per_fold_aggregates, datetime_derived, and text_svd
were retained as the highest-value groups.

The final pruned spec retains **36 feature columns**: 11 datetime-derived, 21 text-SVD,
4 per-fold target aggregates.

All transformations were fitted on the training split only (per-fold target aggregates
and the TF-IDF/SVD transformer were fit on training-period data, with full-train refit
applied only to the prediction matrix per `analysis_plan.json.feature_plan.rationale`).

## 5. Validation Strategy

`validation_strategy.json` (`mode=schema_review`): **`group_time_split`**, chosen
because train periods (77 monthly `period_id` values, 2019-01..2025-05) and prediction
periods (6 monthly periods, 2025-06..2025-12) are disjoint and chronological, in a
panel structured as (`period_id` × `jurisdiction` × `overdose_category`).

**Holdout parameters** (`validation_strategy.json.holdout_parameters`):
- `time_column` = `period_id`, `group_column` = `period_id`
- `n_splits` = 5
- `random_state` = 42

GroupKFold over `period_id` (77 groups) guarantees every fold's validation rows come
from periods entirely absent from that fold's training rows, directly reproducing the
train/prediction period disjointness, while also yielding 5 folds (more stable OOF)
versus a single last-slice holdout. `block_mae` is computed per `period_id` block in
both OOF scoring and the hidden leaderboard.

**Leakage risks recorded**:
- `rate_per_10000_ed_visits` appears as a placeholder constant in
  `sample_submission.csv` — low severity, not a leakage path, but feature code must
  never read this placeholder column.
- `jurisdiction`/`overdose_category` as target-aggregate keys — medium severity;
  any target-derived aggregate must be fit per training fold only.
- The 5 unscored `overdose_category` values (heroin, fentanyl, cocaine,
  methamphetamine, benzodiazepine; corr 0.70-0.88 with scored categories except
  heroin) as auxiliary signal — medium severity; per-fold-only aggregation required.
- Out-of-distribution period range — low severity; hidden periods (2025-06..2025-12)
  are entirely beyond max training period (2025-05), so GroupKFold cannot fully
  replicate true future extrapolation.

**Validation limitation** (verbatim): "GroupKFold over period_id holds out whole
training periods but folds are drawn from within the 2019-01..2025-05 training span,
not strictly the final periods — it does not fully capture pure forward extrapolation
to 2025-06..2025-12 ... Trend/seasonality extrapolation risk for the final 6-month
horizon is therefore only partially represented by OOF error on these folds."

All 31,416 training rows were scored OOF on this canonical fold assignment
(`{run_id}_cv_folds.json`).

## 8. Overfitting and Generalization Controls

**8.1 Validation strategy rationale.** The schema-review guardian detected a panel
structure (`has_time=true`, `has_group=true`, `has_panel=true`, `temporal_overlap=false`)
with disjoint, chronological train/prediction periods. `group_time_split`
(GroupKFold on `period_id`, 5 splits) was chosen over a plain time-based holdout
because it "guarantees every fold's validation rows come from periods entirely
absent from that fold's training rows — directly reproducing the train/val period
disjointness — while also yielding multiple folds (more stable OOF) instead of a
single last-slice holdout."

**8.2 Train vs validation gap.** `train_val_gap = null` in
`model_performance_review.json` — no per-candidate train score was logged by the
specialist agents in either round, so the gap cannot be classified as
acceptable/moderate/high. The reviewer assigned `overfit_risk = "moderate"`
specifically because this gap is unmeasured, not because a measured gap exceeded
a threshold. No `adjusted_robust_score`/robust-penalization field exists for this run.

**8.3 Number of validation splits.** `validation_strategy.json.holdout_parameters.n_splits
= 5`, GroupKFold grouped on `period_id` (77 groups split across 5 folds).

**8.4 Model complexity summary.** The selected model is an ensemble blend dominated
(84.3% NNLS weight) by a stacked gradient-boosted-tree model
(`stack(catboost_log+tuned(catboost)+catboost)`), with smaller contributions from a
trees-family candidate (14.5%) and negligible contributions from the floor stack
(0.8%) and a linear model (0.4%). Simpler candidates were evaluated and rejected on
OOF score: linear models (ridge 1.1999, elastic_net 1.1986) and grouped-mean
baselines (1.2421-5.4282) all scored substantially worse than the gbdt-dominated
ensemble (0.7847).

**8.5 Leakage audit result.** `overfitting_leakage_audit.json` (round 2,
`generalization-reviewer`, `overall_verdict=PASS`, `high_overfitting_risk=false`):
- `cv_strategy_validity`: pass — strategy unchanged from round 1, all 4 candidates
  scored on the same canonical folds.
- `cv_holdout_calibration`: pass — round-over-round improvement of 0.054%, "well
  below the 20% single-round target-leakage threshold."
- `prediction_sanity`: pass — 918 unique predictions, CV(std/mean)=0.685, range
  [3.489, 53.670] within train range [0.05, 79.99], mean shift of 0.781 train-sigma
  attributed to elevated `gtrends_fentanyl`/`gtrends_naloxone` covariates in the
  prediction periods (a covariate shift, not a leakage artifact).
- `train_val_gap`: pass — no per-candidate train/val gap fields emitted; relative
  deltas (round-over-round 0.054%, OOF-vs-floor 2.0%) are both "far below the
  30%/50% gap thresholds."

Separately, `feature_audit_review.json` (`feature-leakage-reviewer`,
`overall_verdict=pass`) flagged one low-severity item: the TF-IDF/SVD transformer
for `state_doh_release` was fit once on full train text rather than refit per CV
fold (unsupervised, no target leakage). Suggested as a low-priority future fix,
not blocking.

**8.6 Prediction sanity result.** `prediction_sanity.json`: `verdict=PASS`,
`high_overfitting_risk=false`. All 8 checks pass: finite values (918/918),

(0.785078 → 0.784656, delta=0.000422).

## 10. Limitations

- **Missing data** (`data_profile.json.summary_warnings`, `missing_rate > 0.10`):
  - `train_covariates.state_doh_release`: 29.6% missing (structural — no DOH
    release that period; handled via empty-string fill + missing indicator).
  - `prediction.unemployment_rate`: 54.6% missing.
  - `prediction.labor_force`: 58.0% missing.
  - `prediction.state_doh_release`: 32.2% missing.
  - Note: `unemployment_rate` and `labor_force` (both with the highest
    prediction-set missingness) were pruned from the final feature set by the
    ablation gate, reducing exposure to this missingness; `state_doh_release`
    (handled via TF-IDF/SVD + missing indicator) was retained.
- **Datetime features**: `period_id` is an opaque monthly identifier resolved to
  11 derived cyclical/ordinal features (`period_id__year`, `__month`, `__quarter`,
  `__month_sin/cos`, `__day`, `__dayofweek`, `__is_weekend`, `__weekofyear`,
  `__ordinal_rank`, `__ordinal`); the raw `period_id` string was never used as a
  feature.
- **Model limitations**: the train-vs-validation gap was not measured by the
  specialist agents (`train_val_gap=null`), so the reviewer classified overall
  `overfit_risk` as "moderate" on this basis alone, despite all leakage and
  prediction-sanity checks passing.
- **Validation limitations**: GroupKFold on `period_id` draws folds from within
  the training span (2019-01..2025-05) and "does not fully capture pure forward
  extrapolation to 2025-06..2025-12 ... Trend/seasonality extrapolation risk for
  the final 6-month horizon is therefore only partially represented by OOF error
  on these folds" (`validation_strategy.json.validation_limitations`). This is
  the run's primary forward-extrapolation calibration gap: the hidden evaluation
  periods are entirely outside the range seen by any training fold.
- **Overfitting risk**: classified "moderate" per `model_performance_review.json`,
  driven by the unmeasured train/val gap rather than a measured large gap; no
  high-severity leakage findings were raised by either the feature-leakage or
  generalization reviewers.
- **Generalizability**: Generalization to future data (the 2025-06..2025-12
  prediction periods) has not been validated against a true held-out future
  period; the predicted distribution shows a mean shift of 0.781 train-sigma
  above the training target mean, attributed in
  `overfitting_leakage_audit.json` to elevated `gtrends_fentanyl`/
  `gtrends_naloxone` covariate values in the prediction window relative to train.
- **Causal interpretation**: All findings describe statistical associations.
  No causal claims are made. Per-fold target aggregates (e.g.
  `jurisdiction_category_target_mean`) and SVD components of `state_doh_release`
  are predictive of the target within this validation design but do not imply
  that any covariate causes changes in `rate_per_10000_ed_visits`.
- **Unresolved hardcoding findings**: 4 `unacceptable` literal-column-access
  findings remain in `outputs/scratch/20260612_161004/` per
  `hardcoding_audit_pre.json`, with no corresponding post-audit log available at
  report time to confirm resolution.
